To improve typing accuracy, slow down enough to notice the movement that causes a mistake, practise that movement consistently, then use it in fresh words. Speed comes back more usefully when you can maintain it without a cycle of mistakes and backspaces.

Begin with a completed [typing practice test](/). Record both WPM and accuracy; do not judge the session by speed alone.

## Separate key errors from reading errors

Not every typo has the same cause. A nearby wrong letter often points to a reach or finger-placement problem. Missing a letter in a long word may come from reading ahead too quickly. Repeated word-order mistakes may mean your attention is on the timer rather than the text.

Write down two or three examples after a test. Look for a recurring key, letter pair, or type of word. Do not assume the app automatically diagnoses the cause of every error; its metrics are a starting point for your own review.

| Error pattern | Suggested drill |
| --- | --- |
| Reaching for a neighbouring key | Slow [individual-finger practice](/learn/) |
| Losing the home position | [Home-row placement](/blog/home-row-typing-finger-placement/) |
| Skipping letters in long words | Read the complete word, then type steadily |
| Errors appear only while racing | A calm practice test followed by one controlled race |

## Practise a troublesome movement slowly

Open the appropriate finger lesson. If the movement is specific to a word, you can also type that word in a local scratch document before returning to NinjaType. The main test does not need to become a custom-word editor for the drill to be useful.

Use the same finger each time, keep your hands close to their starting position, and press lightly. Repeat a small set of words accurately rather than entering a large amount of text carelessly.

Try a short drill of one or two minutes, then pause. The exact duration is a practical suggestion. Continue when you can stay attentive; repeating errors while distracted is less useful than a smaller focused session.

## Transfer the drill into normal passages

Correctly typing a repeated word is only part of the task. Return to a fresh passage where that movement appears among other words. Start at a pace that allows you to read the next word without losing the current one.

Complete the attempt even if it is imperfect. Review whether the original error returned. If it did, spend the next session on that movement rather than adding more unrelated drills.

A suggested accuracy target, such as 95% on a practice test, can help you choose a comfortable pace. It is a personal training checkpoint, not a universal proficiency standard, and different modes may calculate accuracy differently.

## Correct mistakes without panicking

When you notice a mistake, correct it deliberately and return to the text. Avoid accelerating immediately to “make up” the lost time; that often turns one correction into several.

In NinjaType multiplayer, mistakes must be corrected as you type, and corrected errors still affect attempt-based accuracy. The winner’s speed therefore does not tell the whole story. Read the final accuracy column too.

Use the [typing race pacing guide](/blog/typing-race-improve-speed-accuracy/) when your practice is clean but competition makes you rush.

## Review accuracy across similar tests

Compare several completed attempts with the same duration and dictionary. A lower raw WPM with a steadier correct WPM can be a useful step while changing technique.

Use [history](/history/) to track results and add your own note about the movement you practised. If your accuracy is stable but speed has stopped changing, the [plateau guide](/blog/typing-speed-plateau/) helps you choose the next variable to work on.
