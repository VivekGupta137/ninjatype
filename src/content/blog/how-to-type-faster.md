To type faster, make your keystrokes more consistent before trying to make them quicker. Measure your current pace, identify the keys or words that interrupt it, practise those slowly, and return to a passage test. Repeat that loop using the same settings so you can see whether the change helped.

You can start with [NinjaType’s free typing test](/). The useful target is more correct text with fewer interruptions, not a large WPM number from one unusually easy attempt.

## Establish a typing-speed baseline

Choose a one-minute test and a familiar English dictionary. Complete three attempts with a short pause between them. Record WPM, accuracy, and the dictionary; use the middle result as a simple baseline rather than choosing only your highest score.

Use those same settings for later comparison. A fifteen-second test with short words and a two-minute test with longer vocabulary are different tasks. Keep a separate record when you change duration, dictionary, or text type.

If WPM and correct WPM are both shown, read them together. A faster raw pace with more errors may not represent better typing. The [WPM measurement guide](/blog/typing-speed-test-wpm/) explains that distinction.

## Find the interruption that costs you time

Watch what happens when your pace breaks. Do you look down to locate a letter? Reach for a key with a different finger each time? Pause at long words? Backspace through the same mistake repeatedly?

NinjaType’s completed-session insights and [typing history](/history/) can help you review performance. Pair the numbers with a brief note about what you noticed; a chart alone cannot explain why a pause happened.

| What you notice | What to practise next |
| --- | --- |
| Searching for letter keys | [Home-row placement](/blog/home-row-typing-finger-placement/) and [finger lessons](/learn/) |
| Many repeated corrections | [Slow accuracy drills](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/) |
| Pausing on unfamiliar words | Steady practice with a broader dictionary |
| Starting fast, then losing control | A longer test at a pace you can maintain |
| Good technique but little progress | [Plateau diagnosis](/blog/typing-speed-plateau/) |

Choose one issue per session. Changing your keyboard, dictionary, duration, and technique together makes it difficult to identify what worked.

## Build consistent finger movements

A stable finger-to-key assignment reduces the need to decide how to reach for each letter. Start with a comfortable home position, use a light touch, and return after each reach. Keep your eyes on the next word where possible.

[Aalto University’s observational study of thirty typists](https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/ten-fingers-not-needed-for-fast-typing) associated faster typing with consistent finger assignments and limited hand movement. It also found fast self-taught typists. Ten-finger typing is a useful learning system, not a requirement everyone must satisfy before they can be fast.

If you already type comfortably, work on an unreliable movement first. If you are starting from scratch, follow the [touch typing beginner guide](/blog/touch-typing-for-beginners/) and introduce fingers gradually.

## Use a short practice loop

Here is a suggested fifteen-minute session. The timings are a starting point, not a promise of a particular improvement rate.

1. **Warm up for two minutes.** Type easy words at a relaxed pace.
2. **Drill one weak area for four minutes.** Open the relevant [finger lesson](/learn/) or repeat troublesome words slowly.
3. **Type passages for five minutes.** Apply the movement to ordinary words. Aim for a pace that lets you read ahead.
4. **Complete one comparison test.** Keep your baseline settings.
5. **Review and pause.** Note one improvement and one thing to revisit next time.

When you can finish accurately, briefly increase your pace and check whether corrections return. If they do, slow down and regain control. Do not repeatedly restart every imperfect attempt; finishing gives you a more useful picture of your current ability.

## Use races to test the skill under pressure

[Ghost Race](/ghost-race/) gives you a pace based on your best valid saved attempt. Use it to practise staying calm beside a faster-looking opponent. It projects an average pace; it does not replay your historical keystrokes.

[Multiplayer typing races](/multiplayer-typing-race/) add friends and shared settings. Keep one accuracy goal for the session, then compare results after the race. A competitive round can reveal rushing, but racing alone will not necessarily fix the movement that caused it.

## Review progress across sessions

Compare several completed attempts, not just personal bests. Look for fewer repeated mistakes, less searching, and a pace you can reproduce. When scores stop changing, revisit the weak-area notes before simply adding more races.

There is no reliable promise that everyone will reach a particular WPM in a week or month. Start with a routine you can repeat and adapt it to the evidence from your own tests.
