**Roughly 40 words per minute is a commonly cited reference for average typing speed.** A good speed is one you can sustain accurately for your task. For personal training, 60 WPM can be a useful next goal, and 80 or 100 WPM can be later milestones. Those numbers need context: your passage, test duration and scoring rules can change the result substantially.

Start with a [free one-minute typing test](/) and record accuracy alongside WPM. A repeatable result is more useful than an isolated personal best.

## What is the average typing speed?

[Typing.com’s typing-speed guide](https://www.typing.com/blog/typing-speed/) gives roughly **40 WPM** as a general average. Treat that as a reference from a typing education provider, rather than a census of all keyboard users or a measured average for NinjaType visitors.

Different groups and tasks produce different averages. A student learning the keyboard, an experienced data-entry worker and a competitive typist are not interchangeable samples. Copying common English words also differs from composing an email or entering unfamiliar names.

If you are asking “what’s the average typing speed?” to judge your own progress, first make the comparison fair. Use a consistent dictionary, duration and keyboard, then compare several completed attempts.

## What is a good typing speed?

Good typing supports the work you need to do without constant corrections or uncomfortable effort. The following are **suggested training reference points**, not population statistics, hiring standards or official proficiency levels.

| Reference point | A useful way to apply it |
| --- | --- |
| 40 WPM | A general comparison point while building reliable accuracy |
| 60 WPM | A possible next target after you can sustain a lower pace comfortably |
| 80 WPM | A fast-typing milestone to test on fresh passages |
| 100 WPM | A higher target that still needs accurate, repeatable results |

If you currently type at 20 WPM, a clean 25 WPM may be the better immediate goal. If you already reach 70 WPM, reducing repeated corrections may matter more than chasing 100.

Jobs and examinations can specify their own minimums and scoring rules. Check those requirements directly. A practice result is not an official qualification.

## What is a fast typing speed?

For general practice, a sustained 80 WPM is a useful fast-typing milestone; 100 WPM is a higher target. These labels are practical choices for training, not universal classifications.

A fifteen-second sprint on short words does not show the same skill as two minutes of unfamiliar text with punctuation. When sharing a score, include the duration, dictionary and accuracy so another person can understand what you measured.

Do not trade accuracy for a larger number. NinjaType’s practice results distinguish raw and correct WPM. Read the [WPM measurement guide](/blog/typing-speed-test-wpm/) before comparing scores from different modes or websites.

## How is typing speed calculated?

The standard WPM unit is five characters, not one dictionary word:

**WPM = measured characters ÷ 5 ÷ elapsed minutes.**

For example, 300 correctly matched characters in one minute gives 60 correct WPM. Actual word lengths vary, so counting the words in a sentence is not the same calculation.

## Set a goal from your own baseline

1. Choose a one-minute test and a familiar dictionary in [typing practice](/).
2. Warm up, then complete three fresh attempts.
3. Record WPM and accuracy; use the middle WPM as a simple baseline.
4. Pick one recurring hesitation or error to practise.
5. Compare another set of tests after several practice sessions.

Use [finger lessons](/learn/) for uncertain key locations and [accuracy drills](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/) for repeated corrections. Once your pace is steady, [Ghost Race](/ghost-race/) can help you practise against your saved best average pace.

The goal is a speed you can reproduce on useful text. Follow the [typing improvement checklist](/blog/how-to-get-better-at-typing/) to choose the next drill.
