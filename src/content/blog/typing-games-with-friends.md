Typing games with friends combine a shared challenge with an immediate reason to try again. To make them useful for training, agree on one skill before each round: fewer corrections, a steadier pace, or better reading of unfamiliar words. Finishing first can be fun, but it is only one way to judge the session.

Open a [NinjaType multiplayer room](/multiplayer-typing-race/) and invite your group. Everyone types the same passage after a shared countdown. You need nicknames and security checks, not account registration.

If you are comparing services, the [TypeRacer alternative guide](/blog/typeracer-alternative/) explains NinjaType’s room-based typing game and when Words or Time mode fits your group.

## Set up one shared challenge

Choose Words mode for a finish-line race or Time mode when you want everyone to type for the same duration. Start with familiar vocabulary, then change the dictionary when the group wants a different task.

For room creation, invite links, entry checks, and rematches, follow the [multiplayer setup guide](/blog/multiplayer-typing-race-with-friends/). This article focuses on what to practise once the group is ready.

Rooms are public and have a displayed player limit, with ten as the default. Someone who discovers the room can join too. Use the ready state to check that your intended group is available before starting.

## Try an accuracy-first round

Agree to type at a pace where each player can correct mistakes calmly. After the race, compare the accuracy column as well as WPM. Your group can discuss who maintained control without changing the app’s winner rule.

In multiplayer, corrected errors still count toward attempt-based accuracy. A fast finish with many corrections therefore looks different from a careful finish even if the typed passage is eventually correct.

After a difficult round, each person can take a short [accuracy drill](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/) or [finger lesson](/learn/) before racing again. The game exposes the problem; the drill works on the movement behind it.

## Use Time mode when speeds differ

If one friend finishes a Words race much sooner than the others, a timed round gives everyone the same typing window. Choose 25 or 45 seconds, or 1, 2, or 5 minutes. The timer ends the race, and final correct-character scores determine the winner.

Everyone receives a long shared passage. You are not expected to finish it. Completion percentage refers to that passage, so a small percentage is not a grade for the quality of your typing.

For a training session, compare each player’s results with their own earlier round under the same settings. That is usually more informative than expecting a beginner and an experienced typist to start at the same pace.

## Change one variable for a vocabulary round

Keep duration fixed and choose a different word list. Try English 1k for a smaller pool, English 5k for variety, or one of the short, medium, and long word lists for a different reading rhythm. Brainrot offers a more playful vocabulary option.

Do not treat a score change as pure speed improvement when the dictionary has changed. First compare rounds with the same settings, then discuss how the new vocabulary affected pauses and corrections.

## Review the results before rematching

When **Race over** appears, the keyboard closes and the results show every racer. Switch the chart between WPM and passage completion. You can hide lines to focus on one player at a time.

The chart uses samples observed by that browser, plus validated final scores. It can show a change in pace, but it does not identify the exact cause of a pause or recover history that the browser missed.

Give each player one question: “What will you do differently in the next round?” A concrete answer, such as slowing down on a particular reach, makes the rematch a useful experiment.

## Keep the session manageable

Once scores are collected, two connected players choosing **Race again** can start the next round. Rematches keep the settings and use a fresh passage. Results last up to two minutes, and the room’s hard lifetime is twenty minutes, so create another room when needed.
