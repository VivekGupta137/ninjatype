**A keyboard cover can change how the keys feel, but it does not automatically make every person type worse.** Its fit, material and the keyboard underneath can affect your experience. Compare comfort, accuracy and speed under the same test conditions before deciding whether it suits you.

Laptop compatibility is a separate question. Follow the manufacturer's guidance about accessories and closing the display; a typing test cannot establish that a cover is safe for your hardware.

## How can a keyboard cover affect typing?

A flexible layer over the keys can change the texture and perceived resistance of a press. A loose or poorly fitting cover may shift while you type, and its markings or thickness may make key labels or raised home-key marks less clear.

Those changes can affect how confidently you find and press a key. The size of any effect depends on the particular cover, keyboard and person. We have not measured a universal WPM penalty for covers, so there is no reliable percentage to subtract from your score.

If you feel you need more force, miss presses or become less comfortable, pause and check the fit. If keys stop registering even without the cover, use the [keyboard troubleshooting guide](/blog/how-to-fix-keyboard-not-typing/).

## How do you compare typing with and without a cover?

Use [NinjaType’s typing test](/) for a small personal comparison:

1. Choose a one-minute test and the same dictionary for both conditions.
2. Warm up with the cover, then complete three fresh attempts.
3. Remove the cover according to its instructions, warm up again and complete three more attempts.
4. Record WPM, accuracy and any noticeable change in comfort or key feel.
5. On another session, reverse the order to reduce the effect of warming up or tiring out.

Use the middle WPM from each group as a simple summary, and compare accuracy alongside it. This is a practical personal check, not a controlled study. A one-off difference can come from the passage or your concentration rather than the cover.

If one setup repeatedly feels less comfortable or produces more errors, that is useful evidence for your own choice. The [WPM measurement guide](/blog/typing-speed-test-wpm/) explains how to keep the score comparison fair.

## Can you close a laptop with a keyboard cover on?

Check the guidance for your exact model. Laptop displays can have tight clearances, and an accessory that fits the keys may still interfere with closing the lid.

[Apple’s guidance on camera covers, palm rests and keyboard covers](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102051) says to remove a keyboard cover before closing a Mac notebook display. Follow that instruction for the relevant device; do not use a typing-speed result to override it.

Other manufacturers may have different requirements. Avoid assuming that advice for one laptop applies to every laptop or to a standalone keyboard.

## Can a cover help you learn touch typing?

Some learners use an opaque cover to reduce the temptation to look down. It is optional, and a cover is not required to learn key locations.

Begin with [finger lessons](/learn/) and a consistent home position. Use the on-screen key map when needed, then return your eyes to the text. If the cover makes those movements less comfortable, you can train the same skill without it.

For definitions and first steps, read [what touch typing is](/blog/what-is-touch-typing/). For a longer lesson sequence, use the [beginner guide](/blog/touch-typing-for-beginners/).

## Should you keep using the cover?

Choose based on fit, comfort, repeated test results and device guidance. A cover does not replace appropriate cleaning or guarantee protection from liquid damage.

If your keyboard works comfortably but errors remain with either setup, focus on [accuracy practice](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/). Changing the accessory alone will not necessarily fix an inconsistent finger movement.
