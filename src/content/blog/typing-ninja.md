**Becoming a typing ninja is about control, not just a fast WPM score.** Can you find keys without looking down, finish unfamiliar words accurately, and keep your rhythm when someone races ahead? Those are useful skills to practise separately.

Here, “typing ninja” is a playful description of a confident typist, not a certification or a separate NinjaType product. These five challenges use [NinjaType’s free typing test](/), finger lessons, and typing games to give each attempt a clear purpose. Choose the challenge that matches your current difficulty rather than trying to finish them all at once.

## Choose your typing ninja challenge

| If you notice… | Try this challenge | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You rush and correct many words | Accuracy before speed | Accuracy across completed tests |
| You keep searching for keys | Eyes on the text | How often you glance down |
| Your pace rises and falls sharply | Steady over speedy | Whether you can sustain a comfortable rhythm |
| Longer or unfamiliar words stop you | New words, same control | Repeated hesitations and mistakes |
| Competition makes you tense | Race without rushing | Accuracy under race pressure |

These are self-directed exercises, not unlockable levels in the app. Keep a short note of what you observe alongside your test results.

## Challenge 1: Finish accurately before chasing speed

Open a [typing test](/), choose a familiar dictionary, and keep the duration fixed. Before starting, set an accuracy goal you can realistically approach. For example, 97% can be a useful personal checkpoint; it is not an official proficiency threshold.

Complete three attempts with a short pause between them. Do not discard an attempt just because you make a mistake. Compare all three accuracy results before looking for the highest WPM.

**Your goal:** meet your chosen accuracy target consistently, even if that means typing more slowly. If errors keep appearing in the same word or letter combination, practise that movement before repeating the challenge. The [typing accuracy guide](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/) explains how to turn recurring mistakes into focused drills.

## Challenge 2: Keep your eyes on the text

Choose one finger group in [touch typing lessons](/learn/). Start with a small set of keys and use the displayed keyboard map when you need a reminder. Aim to make each reach deliberately rather than guessing quickly.

Next, try a short passage and notice which letters make you look down at your hands. Those letters identify what to practise next; they are not a reason to restart the whole exercise.

**Your goal:** find the selected keys with fewer downward glances across sessions. If you are starting from scratch, use the [home-row finger placement guide](/blog/home-row-typing-finger-placement/) first. Keep your hands comfortable and take a break if you feel strain.

## Challenge 3: Hold a steady pace

In a timed test, begin at a pace you can sustain instead of sprinting through the first few words. Read each word clearly, keep spaces deliberate, and avoid trying to recover a mistake with a burst of hurried typing.

Once you have a saved result, try [Ghost Race](/ghost-race/). Its shadow projects your saved best average pace onto a fresh passage; it is a pacing reference, not a replay of every keystroke from your earlier attempt.

**Your goal:** finish with control even when the shadow moves ahead. If chasing it causes repeated errors, return to solo practice and build a comfortable rhythm. A clean finish can be a useful training result without being a personal best.

## Challenge 4: Handle unfamiliar words calmly

A familiar word list can make your movements feel automatic. To test how well that skill transfers, try a broader dictionary or a quote in [solo practice](/). Keep the other settings as consistent as possible.

Expect the new text to feel different. Quotes can introduce punctuation and capitalization, while unfamiliar words require more attention to reading. Do not treat a lower WPM on harder text as proof that you have lost progress.

**Your goal:** identify one new difficulty and work on it. Compare later attempts using similar text settings, rather than comparing a punctuation-heavy quote with your easiest word-list record.

## Challenge 5: Race without abandoning accuracy

Open a [multiplayer typing race](/multiplayer-typing-race/) with friends and agree on the format and dictionary. Give yourself a practice goal that is independent of finishing position: read the whole word, correct mistakes calmly, or maintain a comfortable pace.

After the race, review your result. Did you start rushing when another player moved ahead? Did you make an error on a reach that is normally reliable in solo practice?

**Your goal:** carry your usual typing habits into a competitive setting. Use the [race-with-friends guide](/blog/multiplayer-typing-race-with-friends/) for room setup, then return to a lesson if racing reveals a specific weak key.

## Track skills, not just your best score

Review comparable completed tests in [History](/history/) and keep a simple note:

- **Settings:** duration and dictionary.
- **Result:** WPM and accuracy.
- **Observation:** one movement or habit that interrupted you.
- **Next challenge:** the exercise that addresses it.

History is stored in your browser, so keep separate notes if you need a record across devices or after clearing browser storage. Look for a trend across several sessions rather than judging your skill from one unusually good or bad attempt.

## Typing ninja FAQ

### How fast should a typing ninja type?

There is no official typing ninja WPM requirement. Choose a target that fits your work or study, and check accuracy alongside speed. The [average typing speed guide](/blog/average-typing-speed/) provides context, but your most useful comparison is often with your own results under the same settings.

### Can beginners try these challenges?

Yes. Start with a small key group and a comfortable pace. The eyes-on-the-text challenge is a useful starting point when key locations are still unfamiliar. For a more structured introduction, follow [typing practice for beginners](/blog/typing-practice-for-beginners/).
