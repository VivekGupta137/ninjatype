## What does a medical transcription typing test measure?

Before preparing for a job assessment, check which tasks it includes. A test described as a “medical typing test” might ask you to copy a passage, transcribe audio, or edit a draft produced by speech recognition.

| Test format | What you do | Skills to practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy typing | Type the medical text shown on screen | Keyboard fluency, spelling, punctuation, and accuracy |
| Audio transcription | Listen to a recording and write the report | Listening, terminology, typing, and following dictation instructions |
| Report editing | Check a draft against its source | Finding omissions, substitutions, formatting errors, and changes in meaning |

Medical transcription work combines language and keyboard skills. Recognizing a term helps you type it, but a fast common-word test alone cannot demonstrate that you understood a recording or checked a clinical report correctly.

## What typing speed does a medical transcriptionist need?

**There is no single WPM requirement that applies to every medical transcription job.** Use the speed, accuracy, test duration, and correction rules supplied by the employer or training provider.

If a vacancy specifies 60 WPM, for example, practise toward that requirement using comparable text and timing. That example is not a universal hiring threshold. A score on familiar words may be much higher than your pace on a report containing unfamiliar terms, numbers, and punctuation.

The usual gross typing-speed calculation uses five characters as one standard word:

**WPM = characters typed ÷ 5 ÷ elapsed minutes.**

Typing 1,500 characters in five minutes gives 60 gross WPM. It does not tell you whether the text is correct. Providers calculate net WPM and error penalties differently, so check the rules before comparing results. Our [WPM typing test guide](/blog/typing-speed-test-wpm/) explains the distinction between raw and correct WPM.

Audio transcription adds listening, replaying, checking terms, and editing. Keep **audio duration** and **time spent completing the transcript** separate from typing WPM; they measure different things.

## Medical terminology typing practice

Practise unfamiliar terms slowly in a local text editor. Read each word, type it, and compare it with the source before increasing speed. Start with a few terms from each group:

| Practice group | Sample terms | Typing focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report sections | history, examination, assessment, follow-up | Double letters, capitalization, and hyphens |
| Anatomy | cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal | Long words and smooth hand transitions |
| Procedures and tests | electrocardiogram, ultrasonography, urinalysis | Maintaining the letter sequence without skipping ahead |
| Similar-looking terms | hypertension, hypotension; hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia | Reading the whole word rather than guessing its ending |

Break a difficult word into smaller typing groups, then type the complete word again. Those groups are a keyboard drill, not necessarily the word's medical roots or syllables.

For medical transcription accuracy practice, pay particular attention to similar-looking terms. A spelling checker can accept a correctly spelled word that is wrong for the source. Practise reading and comparing, not just removing red underlines.

If finding the keys interrupts you, use [touch typing lessons](/learn/) first. Return to medical vocabulary once the movements feel more familiar.

## Medical typing practice passages

These passages are fictional copy-typing exercises, not clinical guidance or real patient records. Open a local text editor beside this page, start a timer, and copy a passage once. NinjaType does not currently have a custom-text field for these exercises.

### Passage 1: Report language and medical spelling

> The patient attended a scheduled follow-up appointment. The history and examination were documented in the visit report. The clinician reviewed the cardiovascular and respiratory sections, then checked the terminology in the assessment. The transcriptionist compared the completed draft with the source recording, corrected an omitted sentence, and flagged an unclear word for review. Before submitting the document, the transcriptionist checked spelling, punctuation, and section headings. The report was returned for clarification where the source could not be understood.

### Passage 2: Dates, numbers, and proofreading

> Training record 2048 was created on November 6, 2026, at 09:30. The fictional worksheet contains three section headings: History, Examination, and Follow-up. Copy these practice values exactly: 12.5, 125, 0.75, and 7.5. Check the words hypertension and hypotension against the source; they are not interchangeable. The editor found one repeated phrase, two missing commas, and an incorrect record number. After making the corrections, the editor compared the entire document with the original text.

After each passage, check for missing or repeated words, changed digits, punctuation errors, and substitutions. Record your completion time and the errors you found. A stopwatch exercise does not automatically produce a reliable net WPM or accuracy percentage; you would need a defined scoring method for those figures.

Use a fresh passage for later progress checks. Memorizing one paragraph can improve your score on that paragraph without showing broader improvement.

## How to practise medical audio transcription

For a medical dictation test, add listening practice using a recording intended for training and a reference transcript. Follow the assessment provider's rules for playback speed, replays, formatting, and permitted tools.

1. Listen to a short section and identify its structure before typing.
2. Transcribe at a pace that lets you keep track of the recording.
3. Mark unclear sections using the required convention instead of guessing a term.
4. Compare your draft with the reference transcript, checking omissions and changes in meaning as well as spelling.
5. Note whether the main difficulty was listening, terminology, key location, or editing. Practise that skill separately before another full attempt.

Use fictional or appropriately authorized training material. Do not put real patient records into a public typing tool. For professional work, follow the organization's documentation, confidentiality, and review procedures.

## A 15-minute medical transcription typing practice routine

| Time | Exercise | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 minutes | Warm up and take a one-minute general typing test | WPM, accuracy, and test settings |
| 4 minutes | Practise a small group of medical terms | Words or letter combinations that caused hesitation |
| 5 minutes | Copy a fresh passage or transcribe a short training recording | Completion time, omissions, and substitutions |
| 3 minutes | Compare your work with its source | One specific skill to practise next |

Reduce your pace when errors increase. Increase difficulty gradually rather than trying to improve speed, terminology, and listening in the same attempt.

## Medical transcriptionist typing test FAQ

### Where can I take a free medical typing test online?

You can use NinjaType for a free general WPM test and use this guide's medical passages for separate copy practice. For an automatically scored medical terminology test or audio assessment, choose a provider that explicitly offers that format and explains its scoring rules.

### Is 40 WPM enough for medical transcription?

It depends on the employer's assessment requirements and your accuracy on the actual material. A 40 WPM common-word result cannot establish whether you will pass a medical transcription test. Check the stated threshold and practise the required format.

### How can I improve medical transcription typing speed?

Build reliable key placement, practise unfamiliar medical terms, and review errors before raising your speed target. For audio work, also practise listening and editing with a reference transcript. Use comparable tests to see whether faster typing still produces accurate work.

### Is a medical scribe typing test the same as a transcription test?

The skills overlap, but the assessments may differ. A scribe assessment may involve documenting information from an encounter, while a transcription assessment may focus on dictated reports or editing. Read the role's instructions rather than assuming that one practice format covers both.

No. NinjaType provides typing practice and results for personal improvement, not medical transcription certification or an employer-approved assessment.
