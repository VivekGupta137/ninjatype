To improve typing speed, work on one limit per week: first accuracy, then rhythm, then harder text, then pressure. Raising your pace before your errors are under control usually adds corrections, which cancels the gain. This plan gives each week one job and one check you can repeat in [NinjaType](/).

It is a structure, not a promise. People start from different places, and no routine guarantees a particular WPM by a particular date. If you want a single practice session instead of a month, use the [how to type faster guide](/blog/how-to-type-faster/).

## Before week one: record a baseline

Take three [one-minute tests](/) with the same dictionary and keyboard. Note WPM and accuracy for each, and use the middle result as your baseline. For context, the [average typing speed guide](/blog/average-typing-speed/) explains how WPM is calculated.

Keep these settings for every weekly check. Changing duration or dictionary changes the task, so scores are no longer comparable.

## Week 1: make accuracy dependable

Slow down until you can finish a test with few corrections. Fast typing is built from movements you can repeat, and errors show you where those movements are unreliable.

- Practise 10–15 minutes a day, ideally on most days rather than in one long burst.
- Open your results after each test and note the keys or words that slowed you.
- If you still look at the keyboard often, start a [finger lesson](/learn/) and follow the [touch typing beginner guide](/blog/touch-typing-for-beginners/).
- Read [how to improve typing accuracy](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/) if the same mistakes keep returning.

**End-of-week check:** repeat the baseline test. Judge it on accuracy first. A steadier result at the same WPM is progress.

## Week 2: build a steady rhythm

Rhythm means roughly even gaps between keystrokes. Long pauses before awkward letter combinations hold back your pace more than a slightly slower average speed.

- Type to a pace you can keep, and avoid bursts followed by stalls.
- Look ahead: keep your eyes one or two words in front of your fingers.
- Drill the two or three keys or words you noted in week one, slowly, and then return to ordinary text.
- Keep posture relaxed. Tension tends to slow you down and shortens sessions; see the [posture and hand position guide](/blog/typing-posture-hand-position/).

**End-of-week check:** repeat the baseline. Look for fewer long pauses as well as a change in WPM.

## Week 3: stretch with harder text

Once your accuracy is steady, try longer tests and less familiar vocabulary. Easy words can flatter your score; ordinary text with capitals and punctuation shows what you can do in everyday typing.

- Use a two-minute test as well as one-minute tests, and record them separately.
- Add a dictionary with longer words, but compare results only within the same dictionary.
- Increase your pace a little. If corrections come back, ease off and regain control.

**End-of-week check:** use the baseline settings again, so you can still compare it with week one.

## Week 4: test under pressure

Competitive pressure exposes rushing. Use it to check that your technique holds up, not to replace practice.

- Try [Ghost Race](/ghost-race/) to practise staying calm against your saved best pace.
- Play a [multiplayer typing race](/multiplayer-typing-race/) with friends and keep one accuracy goal for the session.
- If your WPM stalls, read the [typing speed plateau guide](/blog/typing-speed-plateau/).

**End-of-week check:** take the baseline test one last time and compare all four weekly results.

## Daily session template

A session of about fifteen minutes works for most weeks:

1. Warm up for two minutes with easy words.
2. Drill one weak key or word for four minutes.
3. Type ordinary text for five minutes.
4. Finish with one test using your baseline settings.
5. Note one thing that improved and one to revisit.

## Read your results honestly

Compare several completed tests, not your best single score. Progress looks like fewer repeated errors, less hesitation, and a pace you can reproduce. Improvement is rarely smooth, and one slow day is not evidence that the plan failed. If a week passes with no change, repeat it before moving on, rather than adding more hours.
