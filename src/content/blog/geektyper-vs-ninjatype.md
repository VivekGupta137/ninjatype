## What is GeekTyper good for?

GeekTyper presents itself as a hacking simulator parody. Its homepage offers a choice of themed screens, and random keyboard input produces simulated code inside the theme you selected. The site directs users to press Tab for hotkey information.

That makes it a useful service to explore if you want a ready-made fictional scene in the style of film and television hacking. Follow the controls shown in your chosen theme rather than assuming every simulator uses the same shortcuts, and rehearse them before the scene matters.

## What does NinjaType offer?

Ready to try a different terminal? The [GeekTyper alternative quick start](/blog/geektyper-alternative/) walks you through opening NinjaType, arranging the panels, and setting up an Access Granted finale.

[NinjaType Hacker Typer](/hacker-typer/) combines a keyboard-driven code display with simulated process, network, memory, disassembly, and payload panels. Choose a terminal theme, hide or show individual panels, and press Enter or Run in the payload panel to begin a staged sequence that ends with your configured Access Granted or Access Denied message. Fullscreen works in supported browsers, and panel navigation helps on smaller screens. The [Hacker Typer controls guide](/blog/hacker-typer-guide/) explains the interface in more detail.

The rest of NinjaType covers measured practice:

1. Take a [free typing test](/) to measure WPM and accuracy over a consistent duration.
2. Use [Learn](/learn/) to practise finger movements one part of the keyboard at a time.
3. Race your saved best pace in [Ghost Race](/ghost-race/) when your speed plateaus.
4. Organize a [typing race with friends](/multiplayer-typing-race/) in a shared room.
5. Review completed sessions on the [History](/history/) page in the same browser.

Code output and status figures in Hacker Typer are generated visual effects, not live measurements of another system or of your typing.

Choose GeekTyper if you want to browse its themed parody scenes and use the layout you like for a prank or fictional recording. Choose NinjaType when you want the fake terminal and a practice routine in the same place: stage the scene first, then switch to a separate test to measure your speed and accuracy.

For a NinjaType scene, the [hacking prank walkthrough](/blog/hacking-prank-fake-hacking/) gives a simple setup you can repeat, and the [fake hacker screen guide](/blog/fake-hacker-screen/) explains how to choose a readable theme and panel layout.

## Does a fake hacking screen measure typing speed?

No. Both simulators can generate many displayed characters from a few keypresses, so their output is not a meaningful WPM score. Treat them as stage props, not evidence of access to a device or account. Use fictional labels and explain the simulation to anyone you show it to.

For measured training, repeat a [solo typing test](/) with the same settings and review accuracy alongside speed. NinjaType calculates WPM from correct characters divided by five, divided by elapsed minutes, and accuracy includes mistakes you later correct.
