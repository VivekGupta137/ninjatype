The home row is the starting position for standard QWERTY touch typing: **A S D F** under the left hand and **J K L ;** under the right. Return to this position after a reach so the next letter starts from a familiar place.

This guide matches the letter assignments shown in [NinjaType’s finger lessons](/learn/). It covers QWERTY; other layouts need their own key maps.

## Locate the home keys without searching

Find the raised marks on F and J with your index fingers. Place the left middle, ring, and pinky fingers on D, S, and A. Place the right middle, ring, and pinky on K, L, and semicolon.

Let the thumbs sit near the space bar. Keep a light touch. The home position is a reference point, not a rule that every finger must remain pressed against a key throughout each word.

Look at the text, type a short sequence, and find F and J again by feel. If you lose the position, reset deliberately rather than continuing with your hand in an unfamiliar place.

## Use this QWERTY finger chart

| Finger | Left-hand home key | Left-hand practice keys | Right-hand home key | Right-hand practice keys |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Index | F | F G R T V B | J | J H U Y N M |
| Middle | D | D E C | K | K I comma |
| Ring | S | S W X | L | L O period |
| Pinky | A | A Q Z | Semicolon | Semicolon P slash |

These are the groups used by the app. Some people use different assignments for particular lower-row keys; choose a comfortable, consistent approach rather than switching fingers unpredictably during the same exercise.

The map does not list all number, modifier, or shortcut keys. You can work on those separately once letter movement is reliable.

## Practise one group at a time

Open a lesson for the movement you want to learn:

- [Index fingers: F and J anchors](/learn/fingers/pointer/).
- [Middle fingers: D and K anchors](/learn/fingers/middle/).
- [Ring fingers: S and L anchors](/learn/fingers/ring/).
- [Pinky fingers: A and semicolon anchors](/learn/fingers/pinky/).

Read the displayed key map before starting. Make a controlled reach, release the key, and return toward the home position. Avoid shifting the whole hand when a small finger movement will do.

Try a short attempt, review accuracy, and repeat only the group that needs attention. Your first slow attempt is a reference point, not a failure.

## Combine the groups in real words

A finger drill isolates movement. A passage asks you to change between movements while reading ahead. After practising a group, take a short [typing test](/) with familiar vocabulary.

Notice whether the reach is consistent when it appears inside a word. If a ring-finger key is accurate in its lesson but unreliable in a passage, slow the passage down and give the transition more attention.

Use the [accuracy guide](/blog/improve-typing-accuracy/) for repeated neighbouring-key mistakes. Use the [beginner routine](/blog/typing-practice-for-beginners/) when you need a schedule that alternates drills and passages.

## Avoid turning the map into a speed promise

Home-row technique gives beginners an organized way to learn the keyboard. It is not proof that everyone using it will be faster than every self-taught typist.

[Aalto University’s observational research](https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/ten-fingers-not-needed-for-fast-typing) found fast typists with several different techniques, while consistent key assignments and limited hand movement were associated with speed. Use the chart to make your movements dependable, then measure the result in your own practice.
