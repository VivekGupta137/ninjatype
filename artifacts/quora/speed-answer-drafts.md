## 1. Candidate: How can I improve my typing speed?

Unverified candidate URL: https://www.quora.com/How-can-I-improve-my-typing-speed

The most useful starting point is finding out *why* you slow down, rather than repeatedly chasing a higher score.

Take a one-minute test at a comfortable pace. Note your accuracy and the mistakes that keep appearing. Are you looking down for a particular key, missing spaces, or struggling with the same letter combination? Practise that movement slowly, then return to an ordinary passage.

A simple session could be:

- A baseline test with familiar settings.
- A few minutes working on one recurring mistake.
- Another test using the same duration and vocabulary.

Keep your hands relaxed and use a comfortable keyboard position. If speeding up produces many corrections, reduce your pace until the movement is reliable. Speed often becomes easier when you spend less time stopping and fixing mistakes.

For transparency, I’m building [NinjaType](https://www.ninjatype.com/), which has timed practice and accuracy feedback. Whatever tool you choose, compare similar tests and look for improvement across several sessions. One unusually fast result does not tell you much about your everyday typing.

## 2. Candidate: How do I increase my typing speed from 60 WPM to 100 WPM?

Unverified candidate URL: https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-increase-my-typing-speed-from-60-WPM-to-100-WPM

First check whether your 60 WPM is a consistent result on ordinary text or a score from a short, familiar word list. That changes what you should practise.

At a plateau, separate two problems: finding the keys and moving smoothly between them. If you still look down, slow finger-placement exercises may help. If you know the keys but hesitate on certain combinations, collect those combinations and practise them in words rather than doing only whole speed tests.

Alternate comfortable, accurate typing with short attempts at a slightly quicker pace. Review which mistakes appear when you accelerate. Do not keep pushing through a pattern that repeatedly breaks your rhythm.

Retest with the same passage type and duration. Add some normal sentences with punctuation so your improvement transfers beyond a familiar test vocabulary. Keep 100 WPM as an aspiration, rather than a deadline you must meet.

## 3. Candidate: What is more important in typing, speed or accuracy?

Unverified candidate URL: https://www.quora.com/What-is-more-important-in-typing-speed-or-accuracy

For practical typing, accuracy is usually the better foundation. A fast burst followed by repeated corrections can take longer than a slightly slower, clean passage.

That does not mean you should wait for perfect accuracy before experimenting with speed. Try a pace at which you can type smoothly, then increase it a little and observe the result. If the same errors keep returning, practise those movements before accelerating again.

Look at both measurements together:

- WPM shows how much text you produce over time.
- Accuracy shows how often your attempts are correct.
- The types of mistakes tell you what to practise next.

A test that scores only a common-word list is also different from writing emails or code. Include punctuation, capitalization, or the vocabulary you use regularly when evaluating useful progress.

I would rather see a repeatable, accurate pace gradually increase than chase a single impressive score that requires constant correction in real work.

## 4. Candidate: How long should I practice typing every day?

Unverified candidate URL: https://www.quora.com/How-long-should-I-practice-typing-every-day

Choose a short session that you can repeat and give your attention to. You do not need to spend an hour taking the same typing test.

A manageable starting routine is around ten minutes: a baseline test, several minutes on one weak key or word pattern, and a final test. That is an example, not a prescribed amount that everyone needs. Adjust it to your schedule, comfort, and concentration.

Use short tests for quick feedback and a longer test occasionally to see whether your accuracy falls as you tire. During practice, keep your hands relaxed and stop if typing becomes uncomfortable. A tired, distracted session is unlikely to help you notice the mistakes you want to change.

Track results over a week using the same settings rather than judging yourself on every individual attempt. Consistency and focused correction are more useful than collecting dozens of unrelated scores.

## 5. Candidate: How can I get better at typing?

Unverified candidate URL: https://www.quora.com/How-can-I-get-better-at-typing

Start by deciding what “better” means for you. Writing accurate work emails, typing code comfortably, and getting a higher score on a word test overlap, but they are not identical goals.

If you are learning the keyboard, practise consistent finger placement and avoid looking down for every letter. If you already touch type, identify the words, spaces, or punctuation that interrupt your rhythm. Work on one pattern at a time, then practise it in ordinary sentences.

Keep a simple record of a few comparable sessions. Note the duration, text type, WPM, accuracy, and one recurring mistake. This makes it easier to tell whether a new habit is helping than comparing random tests with different settings.

Use races if they make practice enjoyable, but also leave room for slow correction. A competitive score alone will not explain which movement needs work.
