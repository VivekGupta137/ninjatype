## 1. How do I learn touch typing?

Candidate URL, NOT VERIFIED: https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-learn-touch-typing
Access: direct request returned HTTP 403; existence/title not verified.

Start by learning consistent movements rather than trying to type fast immediately.

On a standard QWERTY keyboard, place your left fingers on A, S, D, F and your right fingers on J, K, L, semicolon. The raised marks on F and J help you find the starting position without looking. Use a finger-placement diagram to learn nearby keys, and return to that starting position between unfamiliar movements.

Practise a small set of keys first. Then add short words, spaces, and punctuation. If you keep missing a particular key, slow down and repeat that movement deliberately instead of restarting random speed tests.

A simple session is: a few minutes of finger exercises, a short passage, and one repeatable test. Record accuracy as well as WPM. Compare similar passages and durations; changing the test every time makes improvement harder to judge. Take a break if your hands become uncomfortable.

## 2. How can I type without looking at the keyboard?

Candidate URL, NOT VERIFIED: https://www.quora.com/How-can-I-type-without-looking-at-the-keyboard
Access: no verified search result; browser verification required.

Make finding keys a movement habit instead of a visual search.

First, locate the raised bumps on F and J. Use them to orient your hands, then practise reaching one nearby key and returning. Work with a small group of letters until you can find them reliably. Looking at an on-screen finger diagram while learning is fine; the goal is to stop checking the physical key before every press.

Keep your eyes on the text you are copying. When you make an error, pause and identify the movement that caused it. Repeating the correct movement slowly is more useful than rushing through another full test and making the same mistake.

Expect your speed to dip while changing a familiar habit. That dip does not establish how fast you will eventually type. Start with short sessions and finish with a passage that uses the keys you just practised. Keep your shoulders relaxed and stop if the position feels uncomfortable.

## 3. What is a good typing speed for a beginner?

Candidate URL, NOT VERIFIED: https://www.quora.com/What-is-a-good-typing-speed-for-a-beginner
Access: no verified search result; browser verification required.

For a beginner, a useful typing speed is one you can maintain accurately without strain. A single WPM number does not tell you whether you are building a reliable skill.

Your score changes with the test. A familiar list of short words can feel much easier than a paragraph with capital letters, punctuation, and unfamiliar vocabulary. A short burst can also produce a different result from a longer session.

Establish your own baseline with a repeatable one-minute test. Note WPM, accuracy, and the mistakes you keep making. If you currently manage 20 WPM, for example, working toward a steady 25 WPM with similar or better accuracy is a clearer next step than comparing yourself with someone posting a 100-WPM screenshot. Those numbers are examples, not universal beginner benchmarks.

Mix basic finger practice with complete passages. Once movements become reliable, increase pace gradually and check whether accuracy falls.

## 4. What is the average typing speed?

Candidate URL, NOT VERIFIED: https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-average-typing-speed
Access: direct request returned HTTP 403; existence/title not verified.

An average is only meaningful when you know who was tested and how the test worked. Casual computer users, trained typists, students, and people entering numerical data are different groups.

You may see roughly 40 WPM quoted as a general reference in typing advice, but it should not be treated as a measured average for every population or every typing website. It also is not a requirement that every beginner should meet immediately.

Test conditions matter. A short common-word list, a paragraph with punctuation, and a long accuracy-focused exercise can give the same person different scores. Some tools also report gross and corrected speeds differently.

If you want to understand your own speed, use a consistent duration and comparable text. Take several attempts, record accuracy too, and look at the typical result rather than selecting only the highest score. Longer tests can help reveal whether your pace changes as you tire.

## 5. Which is the best website to learn typing?

The best choice depends on whether you need to learn key positions or practise movements you already know.

If you are starting from scratch, look for structured lessons, a clear finger-placement guide, and immediate error feedback. A speed-test page alone may measure your current skill without explaining how to improve a particular movement.

Once you can find the keys, look for adjustable text and test lengths, visible accuracy, and a way to review completed attempts. Games and races can help make practice more engaging, but they should complement slower work on recurring mistakes.

A useful routine is to take a baseline test, choose one problem to work on, practise it deliberately, and then retest with the same settings. Keep the tool that makes that routine easiest for you to repeat. You do not need to keep switching websites to chase a higher-looking score.
